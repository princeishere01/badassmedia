Part 1

Understanding the opportunity

What's the actual business problem?

Too Yumm doesn't have a reach problem or a money problem. It has a format problem.

The brand has been running the same playbook (pay a creator, get a post, move on) for long enough that the format itself has gone invisible. Piling more budget onto the same model of buying views won't fix slowing engagement, because the audience has already learned to scroll past it.

The clearest evidence of this sits in the brand's own numbers. The recent Ananya Panday collaboration pulled 1.7 million views but a comparatively thin 20,000 likes, and the response was to boost the post rather than fix the content. That's treating a creative problem as a media-spend problem.

The real gap is that nothing Too Yumm makes right now compounds. Every campaign is a one-off collaboration that starts from zero, with no owned format, face, or tone that audiences come to expect and return for. Two new launches in four months makes this urgent: without something people are already anticipating, both products will land inside the same tired scroll pattern the current content is already struggling against.

❓ What would you ask before recommending an idea?
  1. Is it reach or engagement that's actually declining, and on which platforms specifically? The two call for different fixes.
  2. What's the real timeline and testing budget for the two launches, and how much of it is genuinely set aside for something beyond a standard media plan?
  3. What's been tried before that showed some promise but got shelved? There's often a half-proven idea worth reviving instead of starting cold.
  4. Are there brand-safety, legal, or category constraints, past creator controversies, exclusivity clauses, regional sensitivities, that would rule certain creators or formats out early?
  5. Is this budget meant to move a sales and trial metric, or a brand-perception metric? Those two goals call for genuinely different content.
💡 What opportunities might the client have missed?
1

They're spending to buy reach with nothing that compounds. Every rupee resets to zero once a campaign ends, instead of building toward an asset that keeps paying off.

2

Their own channels are being treated as an afterthought to influencer content instead of a distribution asset in their own right. Lay's is proof that even a brand with a huge following can't buy its way out of a stale content model; the fix isn't a bigger creator, it's a format people actively choose to follow.

3

There's unclaimed space in low-effort, high-relatability content. A real personality snacking on camera with no obvious brand brief reads far more convincing than another paid, polished collaboration, and it's exactly the kind of content the current strategy skips over.

An everyday-snacking thread: a creator talks about daily life while naturally holding and eating Too Yumm chips, not performing a scripted brand moment.

The idea

Build a recurring, in-house content property for Too Yumm.

It runs alongside, not instead of, a sharper and smaller creator layer. Two engines, working together.

🚀 10–15 creator collabs a month, chosen for fit
🏠 Two engines, working together
  • →
    An in-house content arm. Badass Media scripts and ships trend-responsive short-form content directly through Too Yumm's own pages, on a live, always-on cadence, without waiting on a creator's calendar or diluting the message through someone else's voice.
  • →
    A tighter creator layer. 10 to 15 collaborations a month, chosen for fit rather than follower count, plus 2 to 3 anchor videos a month built around one or two consistent faces, so the brand starts to own a recognizable format instead of renting a new one every time.

Two directions worth testing early: a trend-driven format where a well-known face narrates their own Too Yumm moment, adapting a hook like "why does this pack never seem to finish" into a proper recurring bit, or leaning into the chips-and-Coke-Zero pairing trend that's already forming organically.

Alongside that, a loose, relatable "everyday snacking" thread follows one creator's daily life, with Too Yumm woven in the way it would actually show up in a real routine, not as a scripted brand moment.

✅ Why this solves the business problem

It directly answers the format problem from Part 1. Instead of another campaign that starts from zero, Too Yumm builds a recognizable, recurring presence that compounds month over month.

This won't spike sales overnight on its own, and it isn't meant to, but it builds the kind of reach and recall a one-off influencer buy never does. It gives Too Yumm real content in market before both new launches, instead of building from scratch under launch pressure.

📡 Distribution plan
  • Owned channels lead: Instagram Reels and YouTube Shorts become the primary always-on feed, since this is where in-house content lives without depending on a creator's audience to carry it.
  • Creator content is reshared through Too Yumm's own pages within 24 to 48 hours of going live, extending its life well past the creator's own feed.
  • Paid boosting is reserved for only the top-performing fifth (20%) of organic posts each month, identified by early engagement rather than spent blindly across everything. This directly fixes the pattern flagged in the competitor research: a mediocre organic post propped up with paid spend instead of improved content.
  • A lightweight trend-response pod, in-house plus agency, can turn around a topical video within 24 to 48 hours. Navratri, Diwali and cricket season are genuinely time-sensitive opportunities.

Sell the idea

Written as five slides, matching what would actually go in front of the client.

Slide 1: Why this beats a regular influencer campaign

A regular influencer campaign buys attention for exactly as long as it's paid for. This model builds a format Too Yumm owns, so results compound month over month instead of resetting to zero with every new brief.

Run consistently, it does the job of the always-on media plan the brand is currently missing, while keeping the flexibility to burst around the two new launches specifically.

Slide 2: Expected business outcomes
  • Reach and recall that outlasts a single campaign, since content lives on owned channels indefinitely instead of vanishing after the boost window closes.
  • No new spend required just to keep views up, since paid boosting targets organic winners instead of propping up every post.
  • Two ready-made launch vehicles: by the time the new products ship, there's already an audience and a format waiting for them.

Outbound thinking

Who would you approach?

The right first door is whoever owns organic and social strategy day to day, most likely titled Head of Marketing, Senior Brand Manager, or Digital and Content Lead at Too Yumm (Guiltfree Industries, part of the RP-Sanjiv Goenka Group). This is the person who owns the reach and engagement KPIs this idea is built to fix, and who can greenlight a pilot without needing it to clear the whole organization first.

Account context from the assessment: Rajeev Khandelwal, CEO of Guiltfree Industries, is worth knowing and worth being visible to over time, but he isn't the first door for a campaign-level idea like this; that decision typically sits a level or two below him. Too Yumm's current integrated brand partner is Curativity, appointed in early 2024 to shape brand positioning across the portfolio. The right framing is a complementary, creator-first proposal that solves a specific gap, not a full agency review pitch that steps on an existing mandate.

How would you find them?
  • Search LinkedIn (Sales Navigator, if available) filtered by company, Guiltfree Industries or Too Yumm, and title containing Marketing or Brand.
  • Cross-check against recent trade press (afaqs!, exchange4media, Campaign India), since Too Yumm's marketing leadership has changed hands more than once in the last few years, and a quick search confirms who currently holds the seat.
  • Check who is tagged or credited on Too Yumm's own Instagram and LinkedIn posts, and on any coverage of the two upcoming launches; brand and PR contacts are often named there directly.
  • Look for a warm path first. A mutual connection through Badass Media's existing FMCG roster is worth more than a cold request.
First cold email

Written direct, blunt, and evidence-first, in the spirit of an Alex Hormozi pitch: lead with the number, name the real problem, make one clear ask.

Every FMCG brand in this category is running the same playbook right now: pay a creator, post once, hope. Your feed and your competitors' feeds are starting to look identical. None of it compounds, so every campaign starts from zero.

Not a bigger influencer buy. An in-house content engine you don't have to rent every month, backed by 10 to 15 sharper (not just bigger) creator collaborations, that actually builds something people choose to follow instead of scroll past.

We've built this exact model for Zomato, ITC, Angel One, Zepto and Amazon. It works because it survives past the campaign that funded it.
